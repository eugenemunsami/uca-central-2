How We Work

Our delivery workflow, the cadences that keep it on track, and how we run it in Central.

1Purpose & how to use this document

The single, plain-language guide to how we deliver at UCA — and how that work lives in Central, our programme-management app.

It exists so everyone, in every role, shares the same picture of what good looks like: what happens at each stage, who owns it, by when, and exactly what to do in Central. It is both a reference and a standard. Treat its cadences and time limits as the way we work, not suggestions.

How to read it

  • Start with your role. Section 12 has a short playbook for Consultants, ManCo and ExCo. Read yours closely; skim the others so you know how the baton passes.
  • Live by the two tables. The Cadence at a glance (§5) and the SLA standard (§6) are the heart of this document.
  • When in doubt, act in Central. Every instruction here ends in a concrete Central action.
The golden rule

Central is the single source of truth. Funders, sponsors and aggregators increasingly look at it directly. The note you write today is the audit trail tomorrow — so keep it honest, current and shared. If it isn't in Central, it didn't happen.

2Our five commitments

Everything in this manual comes back to five simple commitments.

1
One record.Central is where the truth lives — not a spreadsheet, an inbox or a WhatsApp. Update Central first.
2
One owner.At every step, exactly one person owns the next action, and Central shows who. Work never sits with 'nobody'.
3
Honest, current, shared.Status reflects reality even when it's red. A stale record is worse than a red one.
4
Move on time.We work to cadences and time limits, not to whenever we get to it. On-time beats perfect-but-late.
5
Escalate early.Asking for help is the system working. A red sitting silent for a week is the only real failure.

3Roles & the ownership baton

At any moment one role holds the baton — they own the next action. Passing it is a deliberate, logged act in Central that notifies the next owner. If you've handed it on, you don't chase by email; you trust the system and check Central.

ConsultantDelivery. Runs Ember360 onboarding, clears discovery, delivers interventions, logs weekly updates, requests close-out, raises escalations.Work is in delivery — the default holder for most active work.
ManCoVerification & unblocking. Assigns work, verifies or returns close-outs, owns escalations handed up, runs the weekly review.A close-out is awaiting verification, or an escalation has been handed up.
ExCoOversight & sign-off. Signs off the highest-risk items and major-programme close-outs.An item needs executive sign-off or a decision above ManCo's authority.
Sponsor / AggregatorExternal partners (e.g. BEE123, a standalone sponsor). Own steps handed out to them.An onboarding or escalation has been handed out to them to act.
Rule of thumb

If you are the owner, the ball is in your court — do the next action or hand it on with a note. If you've handed it on, Central has already told the next owner.

How the teeth work

Nothing here relies on someone chasing you. Central makes misses self-evident: overdue items go red, unacknowledged work surfaces, stale updates show on every dashboard.

7Daily rhythm — My Work

My Work is your home base and the first thing you open each day. It collects everything Central is waiting on you for, in the order you should act. Clear it top to bottom and you've done your Central day.

The four kinds of item

  • Acknowledge — new assignments. Acknowledge to accept the baton (SLA: 2 days). Until you do, it isn't truly yours.
  • Discovery checks — interventions waiting on you to confirm discovery and go live (SLA: 5 days).
  • Updates due — live interventions that need this week's update before they drift red.
  • Close-outs — finished work waiting for you to request sign-off, or items ManCo has returned to you.

Your daily loop

  1. Open My Work first thing.
  2. Acknowledge anything new.
  3. Clear discovery checks that are ready to start.
  4. Action every red item — post an honest update, or escalate if you're blocked.
  5. Log what you did today while it's fresh.
Treat My Work like an inbox

Clear it, don't just look at it. If My Work is empty at the end of the day, you are current — the single best signal that you're on top of your book.

8Weekly rhythm — updates drive RAG

The one habit that keeps Central trustworthy is the weekly update. Every live intervention gets at least one update every 7 days. Miss it and Central drifts the intervention to red on its own at about 10 days — and red rolls up to the whole business and onto every dashboard.

What a good update contains

  • Completed work — what actually moved since last week.
  • In progress — what's underway, and any blocker.
  • Written for an outsider — a sponsor should understand it. Specific and dated beats long and vague.

Good  "Held session 3 of 6 with X; drafting the marketing brief; awaiting logo assets from the client."    Weak  "ongoing."

How RAG is decided

  • Green — on track, updated recently.
  • Amber — needs attention or a decision soon.
  • Red — overdue, stalled or flagged. A live intervention with no update for ~10 days goes red by itself.

You can override RAG with a written reason when the automatic read is genuinely unfair — but the cure for most reds is simply posting an honest update.

9The discovery gate

An intervention doesn't count as 'live' until its discovery form is cleared. Delivery timers — and the staleness rule that turns things red — only start after discovery passes. This stops you being penalised for delay on work that hasn't truly begun.

  1. Created — the intervention exists but discovery is pending. No clock yet.
  2. Discovery check — you confirm scope, baseline and that the beneficiary is genuinely ready.
  3. Cleared — discovery passes; the intervention goes live and timers begin.
  4. Delivery — weekly updates now expected; staleness counts against it.
Use the gate honestly

Don't clear discovery until the work is genuinely ready to start — you're starting your own clock. Equally, don't park work in discovery forever to dodge the clock; ManCo can see that too. Discovery is a real gate, not a parking bay.

10The close-out chain

Close-out is where quality is enforced, with a deliberate second pair of eyes. You cannot close your own work — you request it, and ManCo verifies.

  1. You deliver — the work is genuinely complete and updates are current.
  2. You request close-out — mark it done and request sign-off (SLA: within 5 days of finishing). The baton passes to ManCo.
  3. ManCo verifies — checks the evidence and the record (SLA: within 3 days).
  4. Confirmed or returned — confirmed → it closes. Returned → it comes back to you with a reason to fix (re-submit within 3 days).

When all of a beneficiary's interventions are confirmed, the business moves toward close-out, ManCo produces the close-out report, the client acknowledges, and it is concluded and archived.

Make ManCo's job trivial

A fast confirm comes from a clean record: updates current, evidence attached, status honest. Sloppy records are the main reason close-outs bounce.

11Escalations

Escalating is not admitting defeat — it's routing a problem to the person who can actually solve it. The thing we stamp out is a red sitting silent while someone privately hopes it resolves.

When to escalate

  • A beneficiary won't respond and you've exhausted the normal chase.
  • You need a sponsor or aggregator to intervene or make a call.
  • Something is blocked above your authority to resolve.
  • A red you can't clear on your own.

What escalating does

  • Transfers ownership to the right party — ManCo, sponsor or aggregator.
  • Notifies them automatically; they see it in their queue.
  • Logs the reason and starts a visible, dated trail.
  • Returns to you once they've acted, with their note.
Escalation SLA

Raise it the same day you determine you're blocked — don't wait for the next update. Whoever owns an open escalation acts within 2 business days; if not, it re-escalates up the ladder and becomes visible to ExCo.

12Role playbooks

Consultant

You are the default holder of the baton for active work. Deliver, keep the record honest, and pass the baton cleanly when you need help.

  • Daily: open My Work; acknowledge new assignments (2 days); clear discovery checks (5 days); action reds; log as you go.
  • Weekly: update every live intervention (7 days); request close-out on finished work (5 days); glance at your Dashboard; clear My Work.
  • Onboarding: send the Ember360 invite (2 days), chase weekly, and report-and-hand-back or escalate if it stalls (10 days) — always with a note.
  • When blocked: escalate the same day. Never sit on a red.

Management Committee (ManCo)

You keep the pipeline honest and unblocked, and you are the quality gate at close-out.

  • Assign & acknowledge: assign work clearly; make sure new assignments are acknowledged and nothing sits unowned.
  • Verify close-outs: verify or return within 3 days, with a clear reason on any return.
  • Own escalations handed up: act within 2 days; hand out to a sponsor/aggregator where that's the right owner.
  • Run the weekly accountability review: work the dashboard — stale updates, overdue acknowledgements, reds, overdue close-outs. Follow up misses; escalate persistent ones to ExCo.
  • Produce close-out reports and move concluded businesses to archive.

Executive Committee (ExCo)

You provide oversight and the final word on the highest-risk items.

  • Sign off the highest-risk items and major-programme close-outs within 3 days.
  • Take the decisions that sit above ManCo's authority when an escalation reaches you.
  • Watch the leadership view: programme-level RAG, open escalations, repeated cadence misses raised by ManCo.

Sponsor / Aggregator (external partners)

External partners see only their own slice of Central. They own the steps we deliberately hand out to them.

  • Act on handed-out onboarding (chase a beneficiary, request a site visit, resolve a sponsor escalation) within the escalation SLA.
  • Use their scoped views (Dashboard, Beneficiaries, Onboarding) to stay current on their programme.

14Do's and don'ts

Do

  • Update in Central first — before WhatsApp or email.
  • Write notes a sponsor could read and understand.
  • Escalate early when you're genuinely blocked.
  • Keep status honest, even when it's red.
  • Acknowledge and action within the SLAs.
  • Log bugs and ideas as you hit them.

Don't

  • Let a live intervention go a week with no update.
  • Clear discovery on work that isn't really ready.
  • Sit on a red hoping it fixes itself.
  • Close your own work in your head — request it, let ManCo confirm.
  • Keep the real status in a private spreadsheet.
  • Assume someone else will chase the miss — they can all see it.

16Accountability & review

Accountability here is designed to be visible, not punitive. Because Central is the record, the true state is always on show — which means good work is visible too.

  • Weekly ManCo review: ManCo works the dashboard each week against the SLA standard — stale updates, unacknowledged work, reds, overdue close-outs — and follows up directly.
  • Escalation ladder: a missed action on a red or an owned escalation re-escalates automatically and becomes visible up the chain.
  • Leadership view: ExCo sees programme-level RAG and repeated cadence misses, and steps in on the highest-risk items.
  • The measure of a good week: My Work cleared, every live intervention updated, no silent reds, close-outs requested on time.
